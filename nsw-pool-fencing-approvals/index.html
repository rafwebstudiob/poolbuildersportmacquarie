NSW pool fencing and approvals in Port Macquarie

In short: A new pool in NSW needs council or certifier approval, a licensed builder, a compliant child-resistant barrier, an occupation certificate before anyone uses it, and registration on the NSW Swimming Pool Register.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This page summarises official NSW rules so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice and doesn't replace your council or a certifier. Follow the links and ask the builder or principal certifier if you're unsure.

The short answer

The Swimming Pools Act 1992 applies to pools and spas on residential sites that can hold more than 30 cm of water. When you build a new pool, the NSW Government says you must check approval requirements with your council, use a builder licensed by Building Commission NSW, appoint a principal certifier (council or private), keep child-resistant temporary fencing up during construction, and not let anyone use the pool until an occupation certificate has been issued.

Source: NSW Government, Owning a pool or spa: your obligations.

Approval: DA or complying development

Many new in-ground pools can be approved as complying development through the NSW Planning Portal if they meet the state code's standards. If they don't, you lodge a development application with council. Your builder or certifier will tell you which applies to your block.

Local council: Port Macquarie

Your local council is Port Macquarie-Hastings Council. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a pool. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Parts of Port Macquarie-Hastings are covered by flood, coastal or bush fire mapping. Check the council planning certificate for your address, because these overlays can change floor levels, setbacks and what can be built.

The barrier and the occupation certificate

The permanent pool fence must comply with the Building Code of Australia and the barrier standard AS 1926.1. Once it is installed, the principal certifier inspects it. The pool must not be used until the occupation certificate is issued, and a sign saying so must stay up until then. A CPR sign must also be displayed in the pool area.

Register the pool

All pools and spas must be registered on the NSW Swimming Pool Register. If you later sell or lease, you'll need a registration certificate and either a recent occupation certificate or a certificate of compliance. A certificate of compliance lasts three years.

Licences, contracts and insurance

In NSW, a contractor needs a licence for residential building or trade work valued at more than $5,000 (labour and materials, including GST), and there must be a written contract above that amount. Jobs from $5,000 to $20,000 use a small-jobs contract, and jobs over $20,000 a large-jobs contract. For work over $20,000, the contractor must take out Home Building Compensation cover and give you the certificate before taking a deposit or starting work. You can check a licence on the NSW Government licence check.

Sources: NSW Government, General building work; NSW Government, Insurance cover for building work; NSW Government, Contracts for residential building work.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources, your council or a certifier before relying on this summary.
